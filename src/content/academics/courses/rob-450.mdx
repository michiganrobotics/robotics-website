ROB 450 is the culminating senior design experience for Robotics undergraduates at the University of Michigan. In this capstone course, student teams work on real-world robotics challenges sponsored by industry partners, startups, and research organizations.

<TwoColumn>
<div slot="left">

Over the course of the semester, student teams:

- Define project requirements and specifications with their sponsor
- Design, prototype, and iterate on robotic systems
- Apply skills from sensing, reasoning, acting, and human-robot interaction
- Present their work at the end-of-semester Project Showcase

Students gain hands-on experience solving open-ended engineering problems while sponsors get fresh perspectives on their challenges from the next generation of roboticists.

</div>
<div slot="right">

**Course details**

- **Credits:** 4
- **Prerequisites:** TCHNCLCM 350, senior standing
- **Duration:** Full semester
- **Team Size:** 4-6 students per project
- **Culminates in:** Design Expo presentation

</div>
</TwoColumn>

## FAQ

<Accordion title="What types of projects are appropriate for ROB 450?">

Projects should be scoped to be achievable within one semester by a team of 4-6 students. Good projects have clear objectives, involve hardware and/or software development, and allow students to apply skills from multiple areas of robotics (sensing, reasoning, acting, human-robot interaction).

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What is the time commitment for sponsors?">

Sponsors typically meet with their student team for 30-60 minutes weekly, plus attendance at 2-3 milestone presentations throughout the semester. Sponsors should also be available for ad-hoc questions via email or messaging.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Who owns the intellectual property developed during the project?">

IP arrangements vary by project and sponsor. Please contact the course coordinator to discuss specific arrangements for your project.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can we sponsor multiple projects?">

Yes! We welcome sponsors who wish to support multiple teams. This can be particularly effective for exploring different approaches to a complex challenge.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What resources do students have access to?">

Students have access to the Ford Motor Company Robotics Building facilities, including machine shops, electronics labs, testing spaces, and a variety of robotic platforms and sensors. Additional resources may be provided by sponsors as needed.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How are student teams formed?">

Students indicate their project preferences, and teams are formed based on student interests, skills, and project requirements. The course instructors work to create balanced teams with complementary skills.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can international companies sponsor projects?">

Yes, we welcome international sponsors. Virtual meetings and communication tools make it possible to effectively mentor teams from anywhere in the world.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How do I get started?">

Submit a project proposal using the form above, or contact the course coordinator to discuss your ideas. We recommend reaching out early to ensure your project can be included in the upcoming semester.

</Accordion>
