Summer Undergraduate Research in Engineering (SURE) offers summer research internships to outstanding current U-M undergraduate students who have completed their sophomore or junior year (preference will be given to those who have completed three years of study) by the time of their internship. Participants have the opportunity to conduct 10-12 weeks of full-time summer research with some of the country's leading faculty in a wide range of engineering disciplines. The program provides opportunities for students to assess their interests and potential in pursuing research at the Masters or Ph.D. level in graduate school.

All participants must apply online through the [SURE website](https://sure.engin.umich.edu/). Accepted applicants receive guidance by a faculty advisor in a College of Engineering research facility, a stipend of $6,600, attend regular meetings and engage in activities that help prepare students for graduate school.

## Important Dates

- Application Opens: **December 1, 2026**
- Application Due: **January 16, 2027**
- Review Period: January - February
- Offers Begin: Late February - March
- Projects Begin: May 17, 2027

## Selection Process

[Eligible](https://sure.engin.umich.edu/gradadmissions_sure_eligibility-html/) applications will be reviewed by department staff and provided to the Faculty Mentors, who will review your application materials for their selection. It is possible that they will reach out to you directly for further information. You do not need to do anything else or reach out to the Faculty Mentors, but if you have any specific questions regarding a SURE Project, you are welcome to reach out to the listed Faculty Mentor. Any notification of an offer will be sent from the SURE Manager. Learn more: [https://sure.engin.umich.edu/](https://sure.engin.umich.edu/)

## Frequently Asked Questions

<Accordion title="Should I reach out to the Faculty Mentor of the project?">
You're welcome to, but it's not a requirement. It would be a good idea to reach out if you have any specific questions about the project, expectations, and/or simply wish to begin a connection. Any listed Alternate Contacts below are typically PhDs that you could be working closely with, and may also be a good source for information.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I take a class while doing SURE?">
Yes, you can, as long as it doesn't interfere with your SURE commitment. Taking one class could work, but any more would be more difficult to manage while working 30-40 hours a week in the lab. It's also advised that you discuss this with faculty mentor.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="I'm graduating with my BSE in April/May, can I still participate if I'm heading to graduate school in the Fall?">
No, unfortunately. The SURE program is only available to current undergraduate students.
</Accordion>

Additional SURE FAQS can be found [here](https://sure.engin.umich.edu/faq/).


## Robotics SURE Projects

<Accordion title="Project #1: Physiological Sensor Suite for Human-Robot Teaming State Estimation">
**Faculty Mentor**: Dawn Tilbury, tilbury@umich.edu

**Alternative Contact**: Austen Goddu, ajgoddu@umich.edu

**Prerequisites**: Strong, demonstrated programming experience (Python preferred), experience working with sensors (physiological sensor experience is a plus, but not required)

**Project Description**: This project is related to the fields of human state estimation and human-robot teaming (HRT). To enable more effective HRTs, robotics must become more aware of their human teammates, especially in terms of cognitive effects such as trust, cognitive load, attention, situational awareness. While these effects cannot be measured directly, they can be estimated with subjective self reports and physiological effects such as heart rate or pupil dilation.

This project supports a variety of work at the MAVRIC lab by creating a software suite to standardize and streamline both offline and realtime estimation of these psychological effects, and create tools for their analysis. This project is expected to support HRT work at MAVRIC by enabling autonomous robots to adjust their behaviors to human teammates, and to standardize the measurements of how robotic teammates affect their human counterparts in practice.

**Research Mode**: In Lab
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Project #2: Lower-limb Prosthesis Control for Load Carriage Activities">

**Alternative Contact**: Amy Lang, amyelang@umich.edu; Andrew Seelhoff, seelhoff@umich.edu

<Accordion title="Project #3: Modular, Easy-to-Don Design of a Powered Hip-Knee Exoskeleton">
**Faculty Mentor**: Robert Gregg, rdgregg@umich.edu

**Alternative Contact**: Rajatsurya M, rajatsm@umich.edu

**Prerequisites**: Coursework in mechanical design and CAD (e.g., SolidWorks). Experience with 3D printing or machining is helpful but not required.

**Project Description**: Powered exoskeletons can assist walking, sitting, and stair climbing by adding mechanical work at the hip and knee, but only if the user can put the device on quickly and it fits comfortably. A device that is slow to don and doff (i.e., put on and take off) limits everyday use, and a poor fit lets the cuffs shift and press on soft tissue, creating unwanted forces between the device and the user that reduce comfort.

This project will redesign a powered hip-knee exoskeleton developed in the Locomotor Control Systems Laboratory to address both problems. The first focus is modularity and ease of use. The student will use rapid prototyping (e.g., 3D printing and quick-turn machining) to develop and compare donning mechanisms, package the electronics so they do not interfere with donning and doffing, and design removable motor modules so that the system can be reconfigured into different forms (e.g., hip-only, knee-only, or combined hip-knee) in under one minute.

The second focus is ergonomic fit. The student will use 3D body-shape data from large anthropometric datasets (i.e., body measurements from thousands of adults) to guide the design of the thigh and shank cuffs, struts, and hip belt, so that a single device fits 90% of adult men and 90% of adult women in both sitting and standing. Prototypes will be evaluated for donning time, reconfiguration time, and fit on human participants.

The student will work closely with a Robotics PhD student and gain hands-on experience across the full design cycle, from requirements to fabrication and testing. No prior research experience is required; this project is well suited to students seeking their first substantial research experience in robotics and biomechanics.

**Research Mode**: In Lab
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Project #4: Real-World Deployment of Safe Multi-Agent Control of Networked Robotic Systems">
**Faculty Mentor**: Dimitra Panagou, dpanagou@umich.edu

**Alternative Contact**: Marshall Vielmetti, mvielmet@umich.edu

**Prerequisites**: Proficiency in Python, C++; basic understanding of robotics & control systems; familiarity with ROS2; PX4; Linux; Computer Networks; Willingness to learn.

**Project Description**: The project involves deploying and validating planning and control algorithms for safe multi-agent navigation on both real-world and simulated hardware. The student will work with multiple mobile robots (rovers, quads, fixed wings), operating both in lab and unstructured environments. The robots will communicate during deployment over an ad-hoc (mesh) network; a key component of this project will be configuring the networked systems, and ROS2, to support this information sharing. We will implement a multi-agent safe control stack to ensure safety in the presence of other agents, leveraging availability of communication to reduce conservatism and improve performance.

Experiments will be conducted in dynamic environments, possibly containing external disturbances (e.g. wind) or non-communicative agents, to simulate real-world operating conditions. The student will also work with our existing high-fidelity networked-systems simulation environment to implement and fine-tune algorithms in simulation before deploying them.

**Research Mode**: In Lab
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Project #5: Scheduling and Coordinating Advanced Air Mobility Fleets">
**Faculty Mentors**: Necmiye Ozay, necmiye@umich.edu; Dimitra Panagou, dpanagou@umich.edu

**Alternative Contact**: Zhexian Li, zhexian@umich.edu

**Prerequisites**: Strong programming skills, preferably in Python. Experience with optimization, control, robotics, or simulation is helpful.

**Project Description**: In an on-demand air taxi service, an aircraft may be ready to pick up a passenger even when its flight path or landing site is busy. The student will work with the mentors to design policies that assign aircraft to passengers, schedule takeoffs and landings at vertiports, and coordinate aircraft along shared routes. The goal is to shorten waits and make better use of the fleet while preventing collisions, deadlocks, and indefinite delays for any passenger.

The student will review existing scheduling methods and build a realistic urban scenario in [LOFT-Sim](https://michigan-traffic-lab.github.io/LOFT-Sim/) that accounts for flight path execution and limits on vertiport and airspace capacity. They will implement baseline and proposed policies, vary demand and operational delays, and compare passenger wait times, fleet utilization, completed trips per hour, and safety. The student can also explore other emerging research areas in advanced air mobility, such as combined AI/Human operations and resource allocation among competing operators (e.g., Uber/Lyft in the air).

**Research Mode**: In Lab
</Accordion>

**Faculty Mentor**: Leia Stirling, leias@umich.edu

**Project Description**: In this interdisciplinary research group, we bring together methods from human factors, biomechanics, and robotics.
