<Accordion title="Course Registration" id="course-registration">

Wolverine Access is where registration officially happens. Atlas is the tool most students use to research courses and build a draft schedule first. The steps below walk through both, in order.

<Accordion title="1. Plan your schedule in Atlas" id="step-1">

**Resource:** [Atlas](https://atlas.ai.umich.edu/), U-M's course research and schedule-building tool.

You can't officially register until your enrollment appointment begins, but you can start planning now.

- Use Atlas to search for classes and research course details, including grade history and enrollment data.
- Use the Schedule Builder feature inside Atlas to explore options and put together a draft schedule.

New to Atlas? [Atlas Schedule Builder tutorial video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_GPKewtwueY)

While planning, it's worth checking two more Robotics resources:

- The course recommendations below, for what a typical first-year schedule looks like.
- The [Complete Course Listing](/academics/courses/), which shows which degree requirements each course can satisfy.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="2. Send your schedule to Backpack" id="step-2">

Backpack is a feature in Wolverine Access that works much like a shopping cart on a retail site. It lets you prepare for your registration appointment by filling it with the classes you want to take, then, when it's time to register, you select one or more classes from your Backpack to actually register for.

Once your draft schedule looks right in Atlas Schedule Builder, click Send to Backpack. This moves your planned courses into your Backpack in Wolverine Access.

<Callout type="error">
Placing a class in your Backpack does not enroll you in it. You must register for the class, once your enrollment appointment opens, to actually become enrolled.
</Callout>

New to Backpack? [Backpack tutorial video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=swgffKY4W6w&list=PLOBspbWSzXKrecxdmpOqttOI4fFzlJkpr&index=9)

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="3. When to register" id="step-3">

Your registration appointment is the specific date and time you're allowed to start registering, not before.

- Newly admitted graduate students: typically issued a registration appointment around June 1 for the Fall term, or during your program's orientation session, depending on the department.
- Continuing students: issued early registration appointments in November/December for the Winter term, and March/April for Spring, Spring/Summer, Summer, or Fall terms.

Once available, you can view your specific date and time in [Wolverine Access](https://csprod.dsc.umich.edu/htmldoc/eng/dftie/lsaa/htm/sr_ss_viewappointment.html) under "Enrollment Dates" in the Student Center. The [Office of the Registrar](https://ro.umich.edu/registration) also sends an email notification when appointments are posted.

Once your appointment begins, here's how to register:

- Go to Wolverine Access, then Backpack/Register for Classes.
- Click the Add tab.
- If you sent courses from Atlas, click Import Schedule at the top of the screen.
- Select Override Time Conflict and Wait List first, if either applies to you.
- In the Backpack section, check the boxes for the courses you want to register for.
- Click Proceed to Step 2 of 3, then Finish Registering.

New to registration? [Registration tutorial video playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLOBspbWSzXKrecxdmpOqttOI4fFzlJkpr)

<Callout type="maize">
**Good to know:** ROB 501, 502, and 550 will show as waitlisted or closed at the start of registration season. That's an internal procedure used to guarantee every incoming student a seat. No extra action is needed beyond following the registration steps above. Waitlist procedures vary by course, so follow the instructions in each course description.
</Callout>

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="4. Act on waitlist override emails right away" id="step-4">

If a seat opens on a course you're waitlisted for, you'll get an email with "Class Permission" in the subject line. For ROB 501, 502, and 550, expect this email sometime before the semester starts.

Different departments manage the waitlists for different classes, whether or not the class carries the ROB subject code. For example, ROB 510's waitlist is managed by Mechanical Engineering. To request help, email [robotics-sso@umich.edu](mailto:robotics-sso@umich.edu) if you're unsure, and they may refer you to another department's student services staff.

Once the permission email arrives, act on it promptly:

- Verify the class section and class number in your Backpack match your permission.
- If they match, select the class in your Backpack and proceed with the 3-step process to add it, the same steps as Step 3 above.
- If they don't match, remove the class from your Backpack, add the correct one, then proceed with the 3-step process to add it.

New to this process? [How to use a permission/override video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fjZCa_0RVoU&list=PLOBspbWSzXKrecxdmpOqttOI4fFzlJkpr&index=12)

<Callout type="error">
**This step is not optional.** Receiving the permission email does not enroll you. You are only enrolled once you complete the steps above, and the permission is only active for a limited window. If you let it expire without acting, you lose the seat and may have to wait for another override.
</Callout>

Seeing courses full during registration can feel discouraging. In practice, many students add extra courses while deciding, then drop the ones they don't keep, which opens seats later. Patience helps here.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="5. Add or drop before the deadline" id="step-5">

The Add/Drop deadline is the last day you can add or drop a course without it appearing on your transcript as a withdrawal. Through the third week of classes in a full term (or the second week in a half term), you can add or drop freely. After that, changes require a late add/drop request through Wolverine Access, and a dropped course will show on your transcript with a "W" notation instead of disappearing entirely.

Exact deadline dates are published on the [Registrar's Office calendar](https://ro.umich.edu/calendars) each term. For the full policy, including exceptions, see [Rackham Academic Policies, Section 3.1](https://rackham.umich.edu/policy/section3/).

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Browse all Robotics courses" id="browse-courses">

**Resource:** [The Robotics Department's courses page](/academics/courses/)

<Accordion title="What to register for as a first-year" id="first-year-courses">

- **ROB 501: Math for Robotics** (4 cr). Fall only. Required first semester, or second semester if you start in Winter.
- **ROB 502: Programming for Robotics** (3 cr). Fall only. Optional, see the ROB 502 vs. 550 guide below.
- **ROB 550: Robotics Systems Lab** (4 cr). Fall and Winter. Required in either your first or second semester.

If you're only taking two of these three, you may need a third course, such as one in your area of interest, to maintain full-time status.

<Callout type="maize">
**The short version:** waitlist ROB 501, plus either ROB 502 or ROB 550 (not both), on or after June 1. Decide between ROB 502 and ROB 550 by July 20.
</Callout>

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Choosing ROB 502 vs. ROB 550" id="rob-502-vs-550">

Take ROB 550 first if you have a CS major or minor, or an upper-level CS course with a substantial coding project.

Take ROB 502 first if your coding background is mostly self-taught or limited to writing a few lines and calling library functions. ROB 502 builds the algorithms and data-structures foundation that makes ROB 550 easier, and it counts as an elective either way.

ROB 502 must come before ROB 550; the two can't be taken at the same time. It's fine to take ROB 550 in your second semester (Winter) if you take ROB 502 first. MS students can still graduate in 3 semesters on that path. ROB 550 just needs to happen in one of your first two semesters.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="ROB 590: Directed Study" id="rob-590">

**Resource:** [Details on ROB 590 and ROB 690 requirements](/academics/graduate/degree-requirements/rob-590-and-rob-690/)

Not recommended in your first semester. Spend Fall getting to know peers, faculty, and labs first. A ROB 590 Faire in November helps match students with labs for Winter projects. That said, if you look at the [current 590/690 offerings](/academics/courses/course-offerings/current-590-690/) and see a specific project you'd genuinely like to apply to in your first semester, it's worth reaching out.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Special topics courses (ROB 498/599)" id="special-topics">

Graduate students register for 599 sections, not 498 (reserved for undergrads), even if 498 shows open seats. More 599 seats open once undergrad seats fill.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Funding" id="funding">

How funding works, how to find student positions, and health benefits.

<Accordion title="How funding works" id="how-funding-works">

**Resource:** [Full details on Master's and PhD funding, tuition, and funding resources](/academics/graduate/admissions/funding-for-graduate-students/)

Master's students are expected to secure their own funding, through personal funds, loans, financial aid, or a combination. There are more Master's students than assistantship or fellowship openings, so plan to fund your program independently throughout. Some opportunities may open up after your first year, but availability varies and isn't guaranteed.

PhD students are offered a full funding package on admission: typically five years for students entering with a bachelor's degree, or four years for students entering with a relevant master's. This includes a stipend, tuition and registration fees, and health and dental benefits, provided through a fellowship, Graduate Student Research Assistantship (GSRA), or Graduate Student Instructorship (GSI).

Other funding opportunities are listed at the end of the funding page linked above. That list isn't all-inclusive, more may be available beyond what's there.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="GSI, IA, GSRA, and other student appointments" id="student-appointments">

**Resource:** [Full details on student appointment roles, timelines, and the selection process](/academics/student-services/student-appointments/)

The Robotics Department hires students into several roles: Graduate Student Instructor (GSI), Instructional Aide (IA), Peer Advisor, Tutor, Grader, and Building Tour Guide. If you're interested, fill out the department's [student appointment interest form](https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSceqqqvlggXnqCBez7g0JMylt8OK07eowNJ61Q7nAYYoW0Ujg/viewform?usp=sf_link).

GSI applications open twice a year, roughly around July and November, and are shared with PhD students first, then MS students. Guaranteed financial aid students are placed first, with the rest waitlisted by semesters in program and academic performance, which means first-semester placements are uncommon; students further along in their program are generally prioritized.

GSRA positions work differently: they're arranged directly with individual faculty, not through a department list, so reach out to faculty whose research interests you. Like GSI positions, GSRA appointments aren't very common for newly admitted students; they're more often held by students who've already connected with a lab.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Health benefits" id="health-benefits">

**Resource:** [Full GradCare plan details, coverage levels, and premiums](https://hr.umich.edu/benefits-wellness/health-well-being/health-plans/benefit-eligible-graduate-students-gradcare)

If you're benefit-eligible, meaning you hold a GSI or GSRA appointment, or a benefit-eligible fellowship, you're covered under GradCare, U-M's health plan for graduate students, administered by Blue Care Network. It covers medical care with no cost for preventive visits and low copays for most other care, and prescription drug coverage is included.

You'll receive your official email instructions to register for benefits the day after your appointment is successfully processed and loaded into the M-Pathways system. Because appointments are processed on rolling schedules, benefits emails go out continuously as hires are finalized:

- **Fall term appointments:** Processing occurs throughout August, with most benefits emails arriving by late August.
- **Winter term appointments:** Processing occurs throughout December, with most benefits emails arriving by late December or early January.

**Enrollment & coverage:** Once your appointment is active in M-Pathways, you have 30 days from your appointment start date to complete your benefits enrollment in Wolverine Access. Your coverage will take effect retroactively to your official start date.

<Callout type="maize">
**Need assistance?** If it's past these general dates and you haven't received your benefits registration email, contact [robotics-hr@umich.edu](mailto:robotics-hr@umich.edu).
</Callout>

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Full-time Enrollment and Tuition" id="full-time-enrollment">

Full-time status depends on your standing and appointment, not just a flat credit count. A few common examples (this isn't every possible scenario):

- Master's students and PhD pre-candidates holding a [GSI or GSRA appointment](https://hr.umich.edu/working-u-m/my-employment/academic-human-resources-services/appointments-tenure-track-clinical-track-research-track-faculty/graduate-student-instructors-graduate-student-staff-assistants-appointment-guide): 6 credits.
- PhD candidates: 8 credits. See Rackham's [candidacy requirements](https://rackham.umich.edu/navigating-your-degree/candidacy-requirements/) page.
- Robotics fellowship students: refer to your offer letter for your specific full-time registration requirement.
- SUGS students: must register in Rackham only for a minimum of two full (9+ credit) terms. See Rackham's [SUGS information](https://rackham.umich.edu/navigating-your-degree/sugs-information/) page.
- International students: refer to the International Center's [enrollment page](https://internationalcenter.umich.edu/isss/enrollment) for additional requirements tied to visa status.
- U.S. citizens or permanent residents without a qualifying appointment (e.g., a GSI or GSRA position, as above) aren't always required to enroll full-time every semester.

External fellowships (e.g., NSF) and internal fellowships (e.g., Rackham or College of Engineering) may each carry their own full-time enrollment rules, separate from the standards above. Check with your specific funding agency to confirm what applies to you.

Contact [robotics-hr@umich.edu](mailto:robotics-hr@umich.edu) with questions.

For program costs, use the [Registrar's tuition site](https://ro.umich.edu/tuition-residency/tuition-fees): set College/School to "College of Engineering" and Level of Study to "Graduate," then look at the Rackham table (the third table on the page). See [Rackham Academic Policies, Section 2](https://rackham.umich.edu/policy/section2/) for more on full-time study definitions.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Housing" id="housing">

Ann Arbor housing fills up fast, so it helps to know your options and timeline early.

<Accordion title="Types of housing" id="types-of-housing">

**Resource:** [Michigan Housing's overview of on-campus grad housing options](https://housing.umich.edu/graduate-housing-basics/)

- **Northwood Community Apartments:** University-owned apartments and townhomes on North Campus, roughly 1,100 units total, for single grad students and families. A free bus connects North Campus to Central Campus.
- **Munger Graduate Residences:** University-owned, on Central Campus. Furnished suites of 6 to 7 single-occupancy bedrooms sharing a kitchen and living room, designed to mix grad students across disciplines.
- **Student cooperatives:** Student-owned and student-run housing, usually lower cost in exchange for shared chores and community responsibilities.
- **Off-campus private housing:** Houses, apartments, or rooms rented directly from private landlords. Most Robotics grad students end up here, with a wide range of price and distance from campus to choose from.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="When and how to look" id="when-how-to-look">

On-campus options run on university timelines. Munger Graduate Residences applications typically open in mid-April, so if that interests you, it's worth applying as soon as the application is live.

Off-campus housing in Ann Arbor is competitive and turns over quickly. Most students start looking well before they plan to move in rather than waiting until late summer, since the best options near campus go early.

For off-campus listings, a roommate finder, and sublease boards, use [Beyond the Diag](https://deanofstudents.umich.edu/student-programs/beyond-the-diag/), the university's official off-campus housing site (log in with your uniqname and Kerberos password). International students may also want to look into International House Ann Arbor.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="International Students" id="international-students">

Immigration steps and deadlines can change, so treat this as an overview and lean on the International Center for anything current.

- [The International Center's page for new and transfer students](https://internationalcenter.umich.edu/isss/new-transfer-students), always the most accurate and up to date source.
- [Rackham's checklist of action items for international students](https://rackham.umich.edu/admissions/applying/action-items-for-international-students/).

<Accordion title="Before you arrive" id="intl-before-arrive">

- **Get your I-20 or DS-2019.** F-1 students need a U-M I-20, J-1 students need a U-M DS-2019, to apply for a visa. Contact your admitting unit for instructions. Once you have it, pay the SEVIS I-901 fee.
- **Get your student visa.** Canadian citizens don't need a U.S. visa to enter as an F-1 or J-1 student. Everyone else should start the visa process early, since wait times vary by location.
- **Prepare for arrival.** The International Center runs Pre-Departure Orientation (PDO) sessions each summer, in-country and virtual, covering visa interviews, housing, and what to expect. Review the Arrival and Settling In resources as you plan.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="When you arrive" id="intl-when-arrive">

- **Arrive in the U.S.** You can enter no earlier than 30 days before the program start date on your I-20 or DS-2019. Bring your I-20/DS-2019, your visa (if required), and a passport valid at least 6 months out. Your admission letter, proof of funding, and SEVIS fee receipt are recommended but not required.
- **Complete mandatory immigration check-in.** This is required by the Department of Homeland Security once you've arrived. Missing it can end your immigration status, so treat it as non-negotiable.
- **Attend orientation.** The International Center holds orientation events each summer. Graduate students should also check with the Robotics program about any program-specific orientation, even if it isn't mandatory; it's worth attending.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Late arrivals" id="intl-late-arrivals">

If you won't be able to arrive and complete your immigration check-in by the start of the term, reach out to Rackham Admissions as early as possible at [rackadmis@umich.edu](mailto:rackadmis@umich.edu). They can advise on next steps for your specific situation.

</Accordion>
